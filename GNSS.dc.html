Kinematic Correction Methods

Live Correction

Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) enables centimeter precision in "real-time"

A stationary base station or CORS network computes positioning error and transmits real-time corrections to the rover, resolving its position to centimeter accuracy the instant it's measured — no post processing required.

MassDOT MaCORS network — site overview
Requires
A live radio or NTRIP link to the base
Best for
Open sky, cellular coverage, line-of-sight
Result
Finalized position, instantly, in the field
Post-Field Correction

Post-Processed Kinematic: Corrections without a live link

When environments prevent a stable radio or cellular connection, the base and rover log raw GNSS data independently. Corrections are computed afterward in apps like Emlid Studio or OPUS, to reconcile both logs into a single, "survey-grade" solution.

Requires
Independent logging, then desktop processing
Best for
Dense vegetation, interference, extended range
Result
Same accuracy, resolved after the flight

Ground Control & Verification

Absolute Accuracy

Ground Control Points (GCP) anchor digital models to absolute reality

GCPs are known physical coordinates used to tie or anchor aerial imagery to the earth. They are a robust mechanism for ensuring true map scale, accurate project orientation, and "survey-grade" absolute positioning.

Measurable accuracy requires strategic GCP distribution

Deploy 5 to 10 points per site. Target the extreme corners, anchor the center, and capture the highest and lowest elevations. Avoid linear clustering at all costs.

Quality Assurance

Checkpoints provide the necessary verification layer

Checkpoints are coordinates measured in the field just like GCPs but strictly excluded from the photogrammetry or SfM processing software. They exist solely as an independent audit to verify the absolute accuracy of the final map.

GCPs — Active Anchors
Fed into the processing software
Checkpoints — Passive Verification
Held back to audit the output

Delivering the Correction Link

Networked Corrections

Deploying NTRIP

In areas with cellular coverage, an NTRIP caster routes corrections globally over the internet. This allows a stationary base to feed corrections to a network rover or drone via a cellular IP connection.

Offline Hardware Link

Bypassing the internet in remote and offline areas

For dense forestry, remote sites, or internet-denied environments, hardware relies on Local NTRIP (Wi-Fi) or LoRa Radio (868/915 MHz), transmitting line-of-sight over distances up to 8 km.
